Based on the public primary source documentation from the PostgreSQL Global Development Group (**PostgreSQL Versioning Policy**, `https://www.postgresql.org/support/versioning/`), the community support terms and lifecycle for PostgreSQL 16 are detailed below:

---

### 1. Support Status at the Cutoff Date (2026-10-07)
* **Status:** **Supported (Yes)**.
* As of October 7, 2026, PostgreSQL major version 16 is fully supported by the upstream community. It is roughly three years into its five-year support lifecycle and continues to receive scheduled quarterly bug fixes and security patches.

---

### 2. Scheduled Final-Release Date
* **Scheduled Final Release (End-of-Life Date):** **November 9, 2028**.
* On this date, the community will release the final minor release for the PostgreSQL 16 branch, after which the major version will become unsupported (end-of-life).

---

### 3. Support Duration and When the Support Clock Begins
* **Duration:** Under upstream PostgreSQL community policy, the PostgreSQL Global Development Group supports each major version for **5 years**.
* **When the Clock Begins:** The five-year clock begins upon the **initial release** of the major version (i.e., when version 16.0 was first released). 
  * For PostgreSQL 16, the initial release date was **September 14, 2023**. Adding the five-year support window defines its retirement target in late 2028.

---

### 4. Does Installing a Newer 16.x Minor Release Restart the Clock?
* **Decision:** **No, installing a newer 16.x minor release does not restart or extend the clock.**
* **Explanation:**
  * In PostgreSQL version numbering (from PostgreSQL 10 onward), major versions are denoted by the primary integer (e.g., `16`), while minor releases represent bug and security updates tracked by the subsequent digit (e.g., `16.1`, `16.2`, `16.x`).
  * The community support policy explicitly binds the 5-year lifecycle window to the **major version branch as a whole**, measured strictly from its initial release date.
  * Minor releases are published at least once every three months to address security vulnerabilities, data corruption issues, and critical bugs within that active lifecycle. Upgrading to the latest 16.x minor release is standard maintenance (which requires only updating binaries and restarting the database without a dump/restore or `pg_upgrade`), but it does not reset the major version's five-year timeline. 
  * When PostgreSQL 16 reaches November 9, 2028, all community maintenance for the 16.x series ceases simultaneously.

### Unresolved Limitations
* **Upstream Schedule Adjustments:** While the final release date (November 9, 2028) is published as part of the official roadmap on PostgreSQL's versioning policy page, the PostgreSQL release team retains discretion to publish out-of-band updates or adjust specific release days if critical regressions or logistical constraints arise near the final release milestone.
* **Third-Party / OS Distribution Scope:** This assessment is strictly confined to the upstream PostgreSQL Global Development Group community policy. Operating system distributions (e.g., Red Hat, Debian, Ubuntu) and commercial database vendors often maintain independent maintenance roadmaps or paid extended support agreements that may deviate from upstream community timelines.
