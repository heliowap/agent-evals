## Answer

**Yes — under the PostgreSQL Global Development Group (PGDG) community policy, major version 16 is supported at the 2026‑10‑07 cutoff.**

### What the policy page says
The community's own Versioning Policy page states plainly: *"The PostgreSQL Global Development Group supports a major version for 5 years after its initial release. After this, a final minor version will be released and the software will then be unsupported (end-of-life)."* ([postgresql.org/support/versioning](https://www.postgresql.org/support/versioning/))

Its release table lists the relevant row:

| Version | Current minor | Supported | First Release | Final Release |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | 16.15 | **Yes** | September 14, 2023 | **November 9, 2028** |

And the surrounding rows show the same five‑year pattern (17: 2024‑09‑26 → 2029‑11‑08; 15: 2022‑10‑13 → 2027‑11‑11; 14: 2021‑09‑30 → 2026‑11‑12 already flagged as near‑EOL on third‑party trackers). The page I fetched live also carries the site banner *"September 24, 2026: PostgreSQL 19 Beta 4 Released!"*, i.e. it is a page state appropriate to the 2026‑10‑07 cutoff, not a stale snapshot.

### Scheduled final-release date for 16
**November 9, 2028** is the scheduled final (EOL) release of the 16 line — that one last minor release contains any remaining fixes, after which 16 is unsupported.

For grounding the start of the clock, the PGDG announcement confirms 16.0 shipped **September 14, 2023**: *"September 14, 2023 - The PostgreSQL Global Development Group today announced the release of PostgreSQL 16…"* ([PostgreSQL 16 Released!](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-16-released-2715/)); the 16.0 release notes carry the same 2023‑09‑14 date ([docs/release/16.0](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/release/16.0/)).

### Duration and when the clock begins
- **Duration:** five years of support per major version, plus a final minor release at the end of that window.
- **Clock start:** the *initial release of the major version* — i.e. the X.0 GA date (September 14, 2023 for 16) — not the date you installed it, not your subscription start, and not the latest minor's release date. 14 Sep 2023 + 5 years lands in the November 2028 minor-release window, which is why the table's final release is 2028‑11‑09 rather than mid‑September (final releases are scheduled into the regular quarterly minor-release slots).

### Does installing a newer 16.x minor restart the clock?
**No.** Minor upgrades neither restart nor extend the five‑year window. The policy ties the five years to the *major version's initial release*, and the table's final‑release dates are fixed per major version regardless of which minor you run. The same page notes the community *recommends* always running the current minor of your major ("We recommend that users always run the current minor release associated with their major version"), and describes minor upgrades as a simple stop‑server / install‑binaries / restart operation containing only low‑risk, bug, security, and corruption fixes. Upgrading 16.4 → 16.15 (or any 16.x) is good hygiene but leaves the 2028‑11‑09 end date untouched; only moving to a new major (e.g. 17) starts a new five‑year clock. Consistent with this, the current minor in the table has advanced (16.0 in earlier snapshots, 16.15 now) while the 2028‑11‑09 final date stayed constant.

### Caveats / unresolved limitations
- The 2028‑11‑09 date is the community's *scheduled* final release; PGDG dates can shift slightly, and it is explicitly the *final release date*, after which support ends — not a grace period.
- This covers **upstream community policy only**. Commercial or cloud-vendor extended support (EDB, AWS, Azure, etc.) follows different, longer timelines and was excluded per the question.
- Table values (current minor 16.15, "Supported: Yes") reflect the page as fetched; a live re-check immediately before the cutoff is prudent since minor releases ship roughly quarterly.
