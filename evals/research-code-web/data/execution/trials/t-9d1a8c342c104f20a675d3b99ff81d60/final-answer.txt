Based on the upstream PostgreSQL community's primary policy documentation from the PostgreSQL Global Development Group (*PostgreSQL: Versioning Policy* at `https://www.postgresql.org/support/versioning/`):

---

### 1. Is PostgreSQL Major Version 16 Supported at the Cutoff (2026-10-07)?
**Yes.** Under upstream PostgreSQL community policy, major version 16 is fully supported as of October 7, 2026.

---

### 2. Scheduled Final-Release Date for Major Version 16
* **Scheduled Final Release Date:** **November 9, 2028**  
* *(Initial/First Release Date:* September 14, 2023*)*

---

### 3. Support Duration and When the Support Clock Begins
* **Duration:** The PostgreSQL Global Development Group explicitly supports each major version for **5 years**.
* **When the clock begins:** The support clock starts on the date of the major version's **initial release** (its *First Release* date). Following the five-year anniversary window, a final minor version is published, after which the major version reaches end-of-life (EOL) and is considered unsupported.

---

### 4. Does Installing a Newer 16.x Minor Release Restart That Clock?
**No, it does not.**

* The upstream community policy links the support lifetime strictly to the **initial release of the major version itself**, not to individual minor releases.
* Under community versioning rules, minor releases (such as 16.1, 16.2, etc.) only provide bug and security fixes published periodically (at least once every three months) within the major version's ongoing lifecycle.
* While the community explicitly advises users to always run the latest available minor release for their major branch, applying a newer 16.x minor update only updates the installation to the latest bug and security patches—it **does not reset or extend** the five-year support clock or postpone the scheduled November 9, 2028 end-of-life date.

---

### Limitations
This determination applies strictly to **upstream PostgreSQL community policy** as published by the PostgreSQL Global Development Group. It does not account for proprietary cloud vendor policies or commercial third-party extended support offerings, which operate under separate commercial service-level agreements and extended lifecycle schedules.
