## Direct answer: Yes — PostgreSQL 16 is supported upstream at 2026‑10‑07

**Sources used (both fetched directly):** the upstream [Versioning Policy](https://www.postgresql.org/support/versioning/) page (postgresql.org/support/versioning/) and the official PostgreSQL 16 release notes (postgresql.org/docs/16/release-16.html). These are the PostgreSQL Global Development Group's own pages, i.e., primary upstream policy sources, not vendor extended-support schemes.

### 1. Supported status at the cutoff
The versioning policy page's Releases table lists **version 16 — Current minor 16.15, Supported: Yes**, First Release September 14, 2023, Final Release **November 9, 2028**. The page served bore "September 24, 2026: PostgreSQL 19 Beta 4 Released!" and a 1996–2026 copyright, i.e., content current around the early-October 2026 cutoff; supported majors are 15–18, with 13 and older marked "No." Independent cached copies of the same page retrieved by the search engine show identical policy text and the same 16 row, corroborating the live fetch.

### 2. Scheduled final-release date
**November 9, 2028** — per the table's "Final Release" column for major 16. This is the date the project schedules the *last minor release* of 16; after it ships, 16 is end-of-life and unsupported.

### 3. How long majors are supported, and when the clock starts
The policy states verbatim: *"The PostgreSQL Global Development Group supports a major version for 5 years after its initial release. After this, a final minor version will be released and the software will then be unsupported (end-of-life)."*

- **Length:** 5 years per major version, with a final minor release issued afterward.
- **Clock start:** the major version's *initial (first) release*. For 16, the official release notes state **"Release date: 2023-09-14"**, so the five-year clock ends **September 14, 2028**.
- The November 9, 2028 final-release date reflects the project's practice of landing that last minor at the regular November quarterly minor-release slot shortly after the anniversary — the same pattern visible in the table for 13 (first Sept 24, 2020; final Nov 13, 2025), 12 (Oct 3, 2019; final Nov 21, 2024), and 11 (Oct 18, 2018; final Nov 9, 2023).
- Caveat: the policy is "best-effort"; in extreme cases a major can retire early.

So at the cutoff, 16 is roughly three years into its five-year window, with about two years of support remaining.

### 4. Does installing a newer 16.x minor restart the clock? **No.**
Three converging points from the same policy page:

- The support duration is tied explicitly to the **major version's initial release**, not to any minor you install. Nothing molecules the window to the minor in use.
- Minor releases are defined solely as fix vehicles — "increasing the last part of the version number," containing "fixes for frequently-encountered bugs, low-risk fixes, security issues, and data corruption problems." The Releases table gives each **major** exactly one First Release / Final Release pair, regardless of how many minors have appeared.
- The Upgrading section treats a minor upgrade as purely operational — "stop the database server, install the updated binaries, and restart" (no dump/restore) — assigning it **no lifecycle consequence**.

Therefore, whether you install 16.4, 16.9, or the then-current 16.15, the end-of-life date stays **November 9, 2028**; a newer minor resets nothing, it only moves you within the fixed window. (The community does recommend always running the current minor of your major — the rationale is security/bug exposure, not added support runway.)

### Limitations
The versioning page was refetched live from the site; while its dated content (2026 news item) aligns with the cutoff, I cannot cryptographically pin the exact snapshot to 2026‑10‑07. The November 9, 2028 date is the project's *scheduled* final release and could in principle shift under the policy's stated best-effort escape clause. The "current minor 16.15" figure is accurate as of the fetch and is corroborated by the identically cached copy, but any release shipped after the fetch would not be reflected.
