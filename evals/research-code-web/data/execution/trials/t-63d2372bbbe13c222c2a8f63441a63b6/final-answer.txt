**Answer, per the official PostgreSQL community versioning policy** (postgresql.org/support/versioning/):

- **Is major version 16 supported at the 2026-10-07 cutoff?** Yes — the live versioning page lists PostgreSQL 16 as "Supported: Yes" (current minor 16.15). It was first released September 14, 2023, so its five-year window runs into 2028 — well past the cutoff.

- **Scheduled final-release date:** **November 9, 2028**, when 16.x's last minor release ships and the major version becomes EOL.

- **How long are major versions supported, and when does the clock start?** "The PostgreSQL Global Development Group supports a major version for 5 years after its initial release." The clock begins at the **initial (x.0) release** of the major version, not at any later point. For PostgreSQL 16 that clock began September 14, 2023.

- **Does installing a newer 16.x minor restart the clock?** **No.** The policy defines support as five years from *initial release* of the major version; minor releases (e.g., 16.15 → 16.16) are just bug/security fix updates issued at least quarterly for all supported majors simultaneously, with no data-directory changes and no effect on the lifecycle. The five-year clock and the November 9, 2028 final release are fixed by the major version's 2023 release date — installing a newer 16.x minor does not restart or extend support. To keep running, you eventually must do a dump/reload or `pg_upgrade` to a newer major version.

Caveat: the policy is followed on a best-effort basis and allows early retirement in extreme cases, so dates are scheduled rather than absolutely guaranteed.
