## Bottom line

**Yes — under upstream PostgreSQL community policy, major version 16 is supported at the 2026-10-07 cutoff.** The official Versioning Policy table lists the 16 row as `Supported: Yes`, with `Current minor: 16.15`, first release **September 14, 2023**, and final release **November 9, 2028** (PostgreSQL Global Development Group, *Versioning Policy*, https://www.postgresql.org/support/versioning/). The PostgreSQL 16 release announcement independently confirms the initial release date: “September 14, 2023 - The PostgreSQL Global Development Group today announced the release of PostgreSQL 16” (*PostgreSQL 16 Released!*, https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-16-released-2715/).

## How long and from when

The upstream policy states plainly: **“The PostgreSQL Global Development Group supports a major version for 5 years after its initial release. After this, a final minor version will be released and the software will then be unsupported (end-of-life).”** The clock therefore begins at the **initial release of the major version** — for PostgreSQL 16, **September 14, 2023** — not at the release of a later minor version.

Because the final release normally coincides with the quarterly minor-release cycle (the roadmap says target dates are the second Thursday of February, May, August, and November: https://www.postgresql.org/developer/roadmap/), PostgreSQL 16’s five-year anniversary falls on September 14, 2028, and the scheduled final minor release lands on the next quarterly date, **November 9, 2028**. The table labels that the “Final Release”; after that, version 16 is end-of-life upstream. That means at the October 7, 2026 cutoff, 16 remained supported with roughly two years and one month of upstream support left.

## Does a newer 16.x minor restart the clock?

**No.** Upstream support is attached to the **major version**, not to individual minor releases. The Versioning Policy table gives PostgreSQL 16 one first-release date and one final-release date, and the policy text says support runs for five years from the major version’s initial release. Minor releases are fixes within the same major version: the policy says they contain “fixes for frequently-encountered bugs, low-risk fixes, security issues, and data corruption problems,” require only stopping the server, installing the updated binaries and restarting, and that users are recommended to run the current minor for their major version. That recommendation means upgrading to a newer 16.x minor is exactly what upstream expects — but it does **not** create a new five-year window. A system on 16.15, or on a later 16.x minor released before the final release, still shares the same November 9, 2028 end date.

So the correct upstream answer is: PG 16 is supported at 2026-10-07; its scheduled final release is **November 9, 2028**; major versions get **five years from initial release**; and minor upgrades within 16.x **do not reset or extend** that clock.

## Caveats

- The November 9, 2028 date is a scheduled date from the upstream versioning table; the upstream project makes releases on a best-effort quarterly schedule, so dates could shift if the release team changes the roadmap. The public roadmap fetched for this answer lists concrete minor dates only through August 12, 2027, with the 2028 final date coming from the Versioning Policy table.
- Vendor extended-support programs are excluded from this answer, as requested. Some vendors list later dates for PostgreSQL 16 (for example, a search result for Amazon RDS showed extended support running to 29 February 2032), but that is vendor extension, not upstream community support.
