All findings below come from the Kubernetes **v1.34** Version Skew Policy page (primary upstream source, the frozen v1.34 documentation snapshot at `https://v1-34.docs.kubernetes.io/releases/version-skew-policy/), fetched in full. The page itself carries the disclaimer "Kubernetes v1.34 documentation is no longer actively maintained… a static snapshot," so figures quoted are v1.34-era policy text.

## The v1.34 client/server skew rule

For kubectl, the v1.34 policy states verbatim:

> "`kubectl` is supported within one minor version (older or newer) of `kube-apiserver`."

So the general rule is a **±1 minor window**: kubectl may be exactly one minor **newer** than the API server (the client may lead by one), at the same minor, or one minor **older**. The page's own worked example:

> "kube-apiserver is at 1.34 → kubectl is supported at 1.35, 1.34, and 1.33"

(Note that "older or newer" applies only to kubectl; for kubelet, kube-proxy, kube-controller-manager/kube-scheduler/cloud-controller-manager, the v1.34 policy says those "must not be newer than `kube-apiserver`" — so a client "leading" is unique to kubectl.)

## Case 1: every kube-apiserver is 1.34

Applying the ±1 rule to kube-apiserver 1.34 (the policy's own example above), the permitted kubectl minors are:

- **kubectl 1.33** (one minor older)
- **kubectl 1.34** (same minor)
- **kubectl 1.35** (one minor newer)

kubectl 1.32 or 1.36 would be two minors skewed and outside the rule. The policy is stated in minor versions; per the page, Kubernetes versions are `x.y.z`, and the skew policy is expressed in minor (`y`) terms, so patch differences (e.g., 1.34.z) do not affect the window.

## Case 2: HA cluster mid-upgrade, some servers 1.33 and some 1.34

Two v1.34 rules combine:

1. **HA apiserver skew is legal:** "In highly-available (HA) clusters, the newest and oldest `kube-apiserver` instances must be within one minor version." (Example: newest at 1.34, others at 1.34 and 1.33.) So having both 1.33 and 1.34 apiservers during the upgrade is a supported state.
2. **The intersection governs kubectl:** "If version skew exists between `kube-apiserver` instances in an HA cluster, this narrows the supported `kubectl` versions." The v1.34 page's example matches your scenario exactly:

> "kube-apiserver instances are at 1.34 and 1.33 → kubectl is supported at 1.34 and 1.33 (other versions would be more than one minor version skewed from one of the `kube-apiserver` components)"

**Permitted intersection: kubectl 1.33 and 1.34.** Each kubectl must satisfy the ±1 rule against *every* apiserver it may reach, so you intersect {1.33} (valid for the 1.33 servers: same ±1) with {1.33, 1.34, 1.35} (valid for the 1.34 servers).

**kubectl 1.35 is NOT supported during this mixed window.** It is within +1 of the 1.34 apiservers, but it is two minors newer than the 1.33 apiservers — `1.35 − 1.33 = 2 > 1` — violating the ±1 rule against those instances, precisely the case the policy's parenthetical excludes.

This narrowing is expected to persist during the HA rolling upgrade. Note also that the v1.34 upgrade-order section requires "all kube-apiserver instances are at 1.33 or 1.34" as a pre-condition for the apiserver upgrade — i.e., kubectl 1.33/1.34 (never 1.35) is what is certified while you roll servers from 1.33 to 1.34.

## Reassessment after all servers reach 1.34

Once the last 1.33 apiserver is upgraded — i.e., every server is 1.34 — the HA narrowing disappears, because uniform apiservers satisfy the "newest and oldest… within one minor version" requirement trivially. The rule reverts to the simple single-server case:

> "kube-apiserver is at 1.34 → kubectl is supported at 1.35, 1.34, and 1.33"

**kubectl 1.35 becomes supported again** (one minor newer than 1.34), alongside 1.34 and 1.33. So: temporarily acceptable while mid-upgrade only if 1.35 didn't exist for 1.33-era skew — concretely, use 1.33/1.34 during the rolling upgrade, and you may move to 1.35 once the 1.33 apiservers are gone.

- The v1.34 snapshot is static commentary-by-fidelity; I applied its literal examples (1.33/1.34/1.35), which map 1:1 onto your scenario.
