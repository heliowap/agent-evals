**Verdict: not sufficient — two premises are wrong.**

**1. Repeatable Read is not serial-equivalent.** RR sees only data committed before the transaction began and blocks phantoms — stronger than the SQL standard requires (Table 13.1 shows serialization anomalies "Possible" at repeatable read) — but the standard only sets minimum protections, and the docs state business rules at RR "are not likely to work correctly without careful use of explicit locks" ([transaction-iso#XACT-REPEATABLE-READ](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/transaction-iso.html#XACT-REPEATABLE-READ)). Cross-row invariants therefore need Serializable (for all writes and consistency reads) or explicit locks — `SELECT FOR UPDATE`/`FOR SHARE`, or `SHARE`-mode `LOCK TABLE` obtained *before* the RR snapshot freezes ([applevel-consistency#NON-SERIALIZABLE-CONSISTENCY](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/applevel-consistency.html#NON-SERIALIZABLE-CONSISTENCY), [explicit-locking#LOCKING-ROWS](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/explicit-locking.html#LOCKING-ROWS)).

**2. Statement-level retry is invalid.** PostgreSQL "does not offer an automatic retry facility"; you must "retry the complete transaction, including all logic that decides which SQL to issue and/or which values to use" ([mvcc-serialization-failure-handling](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/mvcc-serialization-failure-handling.html#MVCC-SERIALIZATION-FAILURE-HANDLING)). Nothing is replayed automatically. In PL/pgSQL, an `EXCEPTION` block rolls back only persistent state in that block; local variables keep their post-error values, so a handler cannot re-derive the prior decision ([plpgsql-control-structures#PLPGSQL-ERROR-TRAPPING](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/plpgsql-control-structures.html#PLPGSQL-ERROR-TRAPPING)). Owner: application/client or framework; unit: whole transaction, new `BEGIN` to `COMMIT`.

**3. Unique violations: conditional, not unconditional.** Retrying `23505`/`23P01` is "in some cases… appropriate" (key chosen after reading existing keys — an undetectable serialization failure), but "more care is needed… since they might represent persistent error conditions"; retry `40001` unconditionally, consider `40P01` ([errcodes-appendix#ERRCODES-TABLE](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/errcodes-appendix.html#ERRCODES-TABLE)). Gate on `CONSTRAINT_NAME`/`TABLE_NAME` via `GET STACKED DIAGNOSTICS` ([#PLPGSQL-EXCEPTION-DIAGNOSTICS](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/plpgsql-control-structures.html#PLPGSQL-EXCEPTION-DIAGNOSTICS)).

**4. Three-attempt guarantee: no.** "Multiple retries may be needed… with very high contention… many attempts"; a conflicting prepared transaction may block progress entirely ([same §](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/mvcc-serialization-failure-handling.html#MVCC-SERIALIZATION-FAILURE-HANDLING)). A 3-try cap is a policy that surfaces errors, not a guarantee.

**UNVERIFIED:** whether your specific invariant is fully covered by a given lock order/Serializable setup — resolve with a concurrency test on your schema.
