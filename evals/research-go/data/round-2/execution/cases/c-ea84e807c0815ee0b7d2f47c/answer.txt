## Decision

**Minimum receiver changes (before launch)**
1. Verify over the exact raw bytes, before parsing/JSON re-serialization: GitHub computes an HMAC-SHA256 hex digest of the request body using your secret, and warns that the payload/headers must not be modified before verification (proxies/load balancers included) ([validating](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/validating-webhook-deliveries#validating-webhook-deliveries), [#troubleshooting](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/validating-webhook-deliveries#troubleshooting)).
2. Replace `==` with a constant-time compare (`hmac.compare_digest`/`timingSafeEqual`); GitHub explicitly says never use plain `==` ([validating](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/validating-webhook-deliveries#validating-webhook-deliveries)).
3. Return 2XX within 10 seconds and move the 25-second job to a durable queue with a worker; GitHub terminates the connection and records failure beyond 10 seconds ([best practices](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/best-practices-for-using-webhooks#respond-within-10-seconds)).
4. Key dedupe/idempotency on `X-GitHub-Delivery` **plus** your own per-delivery processing state; check `X-GitHub-Event` and `action` before acting ([delivery headers](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/webhook-events-and-payloads#delivery-headers), [best practices](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/best-practices-for-using-webhooks#check-the-event-type-and-action-before-processing-the-event)).

**Incoming evidence of authenticity:** only the valid `X-Hub-Signature-256` over the unmodified body with your stored secret. `X-GitHub-Delivery`, `User-Agent: GitHub-Hookshot/…`, and source-IP allow-listing are secondary ([delivery headers](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/webhook-events-and-payloads#delivery-headers), [best practices](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/best-practices-for-using-webhooks#allow-githubs-ip-addresses)).

**Identifier behavior — premise corrected:** a requested redelivery reuses the *same* `X-GitHub-Delivery` value as the original ([best practices](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/best-practices-for-using-webhooks#use-the-x-github-delivery-header)). It is not a new identifier, and it identifies the event, not the attempt. So dedupe on it *should* collapse redeliveries; treat "already seen" as a reason to replay the *pending* work for that GUID, not to drop it or to bill it as new.

**Retry/timing assumptions — premise corrected:** GitHub does **not** automatically redeliver failed deliveries; redelivery is manual/API-driven and only for the past 3 days ([handling failures](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/using-webhooks/handling-failed-webhook-deliveries#about-webhook-delivery-failures), [redelivering](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/testing-and-troubleshooting-webhooks/redelivering-webhooks#about-redelivering-webhooks)). Safe to rely on: 10-second response budget, 25 MB payload cap, and some events never being delivered (>5000-branch pushes) ([payload cap](https://docs.github.com/en/webhooks/webhook-events-and-payloads#payload-cap)).

**Exactly-once?** No. These mechanisms give signature authenticity plus at-most-once *GitHub delivery*; a crash after your 2XX, or during the slow job, is invisible to GitHub. Exactly-once business side effects require your own durable inbox/outbox and idempotent apply — UNVERIFIED by GitHub docs. Resolve via GitHub's delivery-retention and REST redelivery-status docs.
