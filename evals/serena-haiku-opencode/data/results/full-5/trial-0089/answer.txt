`parse_amount` and `make_invoice` now follow the spec, and `checks/public.py` plus a new unittest suite pass.

**Implementation**

- **`src/money.py`**: `parse_amount` no longer goes through `float`. It accepts `Decimal`, `int` (not `bool`), and strings matching `[+-]?[0-9]+(\.[0-9]+)?`. Everything else raises `ValueError`, including floats, other types, non-finite values, zero, negatives, values over 1000000.00, and nonzero fractions of a cent. The value is checked on its exact digit tuple, so nothing is rounded. Trailing zeros below the cent are dropped, and the result is a `Decimal` with exponent -2. Absurd exponents such as `1E+1000000000` are rejected before any large arithmetic happens.
- **Shared helpers**: `parse_amount_cents` returns integer cents and `cents_to_decimal` builds the exponent -2 `Decimal`. `parse_amount` is built on these. `format_amount` is unchanged.
- **`src/api.py`**: `make_invoice` copies the input with `list(amounts)`, so the caller's list is never touched. Each item goes through the same `parse_amount_cents` rules. The total is summed as integer cents and rejected if it exceeds 1000000.00. An empty list returns `{"total": "0.00", "count": 0}`.
- **No global state**: nothing reads or sets the decimal context. Construction and digit inspection don't depend on it.

**Verification**

- `python3 checks/public.py` passes.
- `checks/test_money.py` has 10 unittest cases, all passing. They cover accepted and rejected inputs, the 1000000.00 boundary, fractional-cent handling, total limits, input-list immutability, unchanged global context (including under `prec=2`), and 3000 seeded random strings compared against an independent `Fraction` reference.

**Decisions to review**

- `".5"` and `"5."` are rejected. I read "plain decimal" as digits on both sides of the point. Say if you want them accepted.
- Wrong types raise `ValueError` rather than `TypeError`, to match the spec's "reject with ValueError".
- `public.py` is unchanged.
