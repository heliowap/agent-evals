# Review of the methodology and results

## Verdict for this cut

The 28 configurations produced 112 valid reviews, one per case/axis/configuration. The re-evaluation kept **73 hits in 112 opportunities** of the [predefined answer key](grading-rubric.md): 56/56 on *Standards* and 17/56 on *Spec*. In the [Click #3773](fixture-research.md) case, 16/28 *Spec* answers explained why the class attribute is ineffective; in [Click #3868](fixture-research.md), only 1/28 identified the splitting of an argument with internal quotes. GPT-6 Sol `high` remains the only configuration with 4/4 and no confirmed false positive. The [consolidated table](comparison.md) shows the 28 configurations, the times and the reported cost.

The count of **verifiably false** claims went from 30 in the original adjudication to **52** after applying the same rule to all answers on both axes. Of these, 42 relate to the first case and 10 to the second. The new result, with citations per answer, is in [blind-rescore.json](blind-rescore.json); it **replaces only the false-positive count** of the `*-scores.json` files and the earlier reports. The answer-key hits, times and costs did not change. The old numbers must not be combined with the new table.

## What was checked

The diffs and the specification/rules excerpts were fixed in [fixtures.json](fixtures.json) before the rounds. Both commits are public and were not merged. There was **one valid answer per combination**, without tools; unsuccessful or contaminated attempts were [archived separately](attempts/). Scoring was not recalibrated to reward bugs discovered after the answer key was defined. An omission or a plausible finding outside the answer key does not count as a false positive.

To recount the false positives, the 112 answers were shuffled per case (seed `20260926`), labeled without model/route/effort and handed to **one adjudicator per case**. Each applied rules written before this second reading: separately mark distinct factual claims that can be refuted in the code or in a reproduction; do not mark conditional hypotheses, judgments or merely undemonstrated statements. The counted citations were checked against the original answers. After re-identifying the models, the main agent withdrew two claims about the real launch on Windows that the adjudicator had counted without sufficient evidence to refute them. This was a **review blind to the model name**, not two independent evaluations of each answer nor proof of agreement between raters. The false-positive rules were formalized after observing initial answers; only the four answer-key items were defined before all answers.

In the first case, `textwrap.TextWrapper.__init__` sets `self.break_on_hyphens=True`, hiding the `False` added to the class. Therefore, saying that the diff **already** changed the output of `wrap_text` is false; saying that it would change other texts **if the setting started to take effect** is a valid caveat. In the second, the reproduction with `WIN=True` on macOS demonstrates the splitting of `C:\tools\vim.exe --arg="a b"` into three tokens, but does not demonstrate whether `CreateProcess` or Notepad fail on a real Windows. A categorical unverified statement about that launch must remain **not demonstrated**, not a confirmed false positive.

## Limits that change the interpretation

| Limit | Consequence for the reading |
| --- | --- |
| Two diffs from the same project, no valid repetition per combination | The difference between 3/4 and 4/4 is a single finding. There is no estimate of variability, hit rate on other PRs or general ranking of models. The comparison between effort levels also does not demonstrate causality. |
| Two explicit *Standards* findings in the excerpts delivered in the prompt | All 56 answers on this axis scored. It distinguishes the configurations little and does not measure the ability to locate rules, nor the overall quality of a Standards review. |
| One *Spec* point per case | Other real bugs of PR #3868, such as the loss of backslashes in arguments after the executable, were correctly left out of the frozen scoreboard. The score measures recall of these four targets, not coverage of all defects nor precision of all comments. |
| The editorial *Spec* text of PR #3773 says the request is about usage, "not all text formatting"; the form requires a *scope creep* section | The wording can induce the incorrect comment that the diff **already** altered all formatting. This especially affects the false-positive comparison of the first case; it is not an isolated effect of the model. The excerpt should be treated as part of the evaluated prompt, not as a neutral description of the issue. |
| Context fixed in the prompt and tools forbidden | The test measures analysis of already-selected material. It does not measure source search, test execution, repository navigation, CI review nor the full execution of `/code-review`. The inherited function that makes #3773 ineffective is not reproduced in the context excerpt delivered. |
| Repository rules and session metadata | The cited template asks for `CHANGES.rst`, but the pinned revisions have `CHANGES.md`: the template's requirement seems outdated and does not serve as a reliable additional answer key. Summed durations are not wall-clock time; US$ 0 on OAuth/`cli_proxy` does not prove it was free, and there are incomplete token counters. The Fast selection of Luna does not prove the effective routing. |
| Blind re-evaluation per case, without double evaluation of the same answer | The strict criterion for internal quotes requires saying that the argument **splits**, not just that quotes are retained; four borderline answers would gain a point under a more permissive rule, although they do not describe the concrete effect. Some effect claims in #3773 also depend on the distinction between describing the intent and asserting a current result. The `spec_lenient` and `fp_low`/`fp_high` fields in `blind-rescore.json` are illustrative sensitivity analyses, **not** statistical intervals nor another official score. |

## Recommended next test

If the goal is to choose a reviewer for recurring use, freeze **new cases before running models**, spread across more repositories, languages, diff sizes and defect types; repeat each configuration, alternate the order of execution and preserve the route and effort actually used. Set aside a prompt variant without the editorial scope sentence of #3773 to measure sensitivity to the wording, without retroactively mixing it into this table. Prepare precision and coverage answer keys that admit several valid findings, do a blind double adjudication of a sample and test the claims about the real launch on Windows. If the decision depends on cost and latency, obtain the actual invoice/credits and repeated measurements under comparable conditions. Until then, read Sol `high` as the **best score on this set**, not as the overall winner.
