I converted `customers.csv` and saved the result as `customers.json`: an array of 87 records in the original row order.

- **`id`** stays a string, so leading zeros are kept (e.g. `"00000"`).
- **`note`** stays a string. Commas, quotes inside the text and accented characters (`olá`) come through unchanged. The 22 notes that span two lines keep their line break (`"two\nlines"`), with no stray carriage returns from the CSV's Windows-style line endings.
- **`active`** is a true/false value. Every row had exactly `true` or `false`.
- **`age`** is a whole number, or `null` when blank (22 rows).

Sample record: `{"id": "00002", "note": "two\nlines", "active": false, "age": 2}`

One thing you might not expect: rows with an empty `note` have `""` (an empty string), not `null`, since you only asked for blank ages to become `null`.
